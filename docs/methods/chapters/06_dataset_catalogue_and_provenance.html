7  Master Dataset Catalogue & Data Provenance

8 Master Dataset Catalogue & Data Provenance

This chapter documents the 23 formal data assets serving the Kenya ENSO Explorer platform. Each entry conforms to the Comprehensive Data Hub (CDH) Schema v0.3.0, recording upstream producers, spatial-temporal coverages, scientific caveats, and open-access licensing terms.

8.1 enso-driver-indices: Ocean Climate Teleconnection Drivers (Pacific RONI, Indian Ocean DMI & Western-V)

Category: Climate  |  Publishing State: authored  |  License: Open Data

8.1.1 Summary

Harmonised multi-decadal monthly and seasonal time series of primary equatorial ocean-atmosphere climate teleconnection drivers governing Kenyan rainfall: NOAA CPC Relative Oceanic Niño Index (RONI) for Pacific ENSO state; NOAA CPC / HadISST Dipole Mode Index (DMI) for Indian Ocean Dipole state; and Funk et al. Western-V SST index for MAM Long Rains teleconnections. Standardised and compiled into analysis-ready parquet tables (1950 to present).

  • Temporal Coverage: 1950-01 to present (open-ended)
  • Spatial Resolution: global-oceans, equatorial-pacific, indian-ocean

8.1.2 Data Producers & Custodians

  • NOAA Climate Prediction Center - https://www.cpc.ncep.noaa.gov/
  • Alliance of Bioversity International and CIAT (Peter Steward)

8.1.3 Scientific Caveats & Guidance

[!WARNING] Attribution: “Data provided by NOAA Climate Prediction Center (CPC) and compiled for the African Agriculture Adaptation Atlas (AAAA).” RELATIVE OCEANIC NINO INDEX (RONI) OVER RAW NINO 3.4. CPC computes RONI by subtracting the tropical-mean (20S-20N) SST anomaly from the Nino 3.4 anomaly. This detrending removes secular tropical ocean warming (~+0.6 degC since 1950) that otherwise causes historical cool baselines to register as artificial permanent El Nino conditions. Always use RONI for multi-decadal analogue selection. INDIAN OCEAN DIPOLE (IOD) DOMINANCE IN SHORT RAINS. The DMI is the primary physical forcing of Kenyan rainfall variability during the October-December (OND) Short Rains. Warm ENSO and positive IOD co-occur frequently but can decouple; off-diagonal states (El Nino + negative IOD) produce subdued anomalies. SEASONAL ASYMMETRY AND LONG RAINS (MAM) DECOUPLING. Pacific ENSO indices exhibit weak correlation with Kenyan rainfall during the March-May (MAM) Long Rains (r ~ 0.08). The Western-V index captures western Pacific warm pool gradients that govern convective subsidence over the Horn of Africa, providing independent predictive skill where ENSO fails. Z-SCORE STANDARDIZATION REQUIREMENT. Pacific SST anomalies (~0.5-2.5 degC) and regional rainfall anomalies exhibit a 10.6:1 variance distortion ratio. Multi-driver analogue selection must use z-score standardisation across all driver dimensions to ensure equal physical weighting.

8.2 africa-precipitation-monthly-seasonal: Monthly and Seasonal Precipitation from CHIRPS v3 (Africa)

Category: Climate  |  Publishing State: authored  |  License: Open Data

8.2.1 Summary

Total precipitation (PTOT) aggregated from CHIRPS v3.0 and delivered as per-pixel monthly Cloud Optimized GeoTIFFs and per-year rolling 3-month (seasonal) sums over Africa, 1981 to present. This is the rainfall backbone of the KE-ENSO Explorer’s seasonal/ENSO analysis: the twelve seasonal windows (JFM, FMA, …, DJF) are the tri-month sums the notebook compares against ENSO phase and against the drought (SPEI), vegetation (NDVI) and flood layers. A derived aggregation product, not a redistribution of the CHIRPS daily archive.

  • Temporal Coverage: 1981-01 to present (open-ended)
  • Spatial Resolution: africa — 0.05 deg (~5.5 km, CHIRPS native)

8.2.2 Data Producers & Custodians

  • Alliance of Bioversity International and CIAT (Peter Steward)
  • Climate Hazards Center, UC Santa Barbara - https://www.chc.ucsb.edu/data/chirps

8.2.3 Scientific Caveats & Guidance

[!WARNING] Source CHIRPS is public domain (“no restrictions on use”); these reprocessed COGs are released CC-BY-4.0 with attribution to the Climate Hazards Center, UC Santa Barbara. Seasonal windows are labelled by the calendar year in which the window ends (DJF_2020 = Dec 2019 to Feb 2020). Edge windows that the record cannot cover in full are absent (NDJ and DJF 1981; windows ending after the latest monthly file), so not every season x year combination of the template exists.

8.3 africa-spei-drought: SPEI Drought Index, 3- and 12-month (Africa)

Category: Climate  |  Publishing State: authored  |  License: Open Data

8.3.1 Summary

Standardized Precipitation-Evapotranspiration Index at 3-month (SPEI-03) and 12-month (SPEI-12) accumulation, as per-pixel monthly Cloud Optimized GeoTIFFs over Africa, 1981 to present. Built from CHIRPS v3 precipitation and a potential-evapotranspiration climate water balance fitted with a log-logistic distribution; negative values are drier than the reference climatology. The drought layer of the KE-ENSO Explorer: the short accumulation tracks seasonal/agricultural drought, the long accumulation hydrological drought.

  • Temporal Coverage: 1981-01 to present (open-ended)
  • Spatial Resolution: africa — 0.05 deg (~5.5 km, CHIRPS grid)

8.3.2 Data Producers & Custodians

  • Alliance of Bioversity International and CIAT (Peter Steward)

8.3.3 Scientific Caveats & Guidance

[!WARNING] SPEI is a z-score anomaly against the fitted reference distribution, not an absolute deficit. Arid-cell -Inf values (zero-precipitation tails of the log-logistic fit) are clamped to NoData.

8.4 eastafrica-flood-jrc: JRC / GloFAS Return-Period River Flood Hazard (Global)

Category: Satellite  |  Publishing State: authored  |  License: Open Data

8.4.1 Summary

Modelled global riverine flood hazard from the JRC / GloFAS Global Flood Maps (CEMS-GLOFAS v2.1.2): inundation depth for seven return periods, 10 to 500 years, at 3 arc-seconds, as a static baseline climatology. Published as Cloud Optimized GeoTIFFs with internal overviews on Source Cooperative, which is the canonical read location. The Adaptation Atlas additionally holds a provisional Kenya crop of the same data. Within the KE-ENSO Explorer this is the MODELLED flood-prone baseline complementing the OBSERVED Copernicus GFM extent (record kenya-flood-gfm): JRC answers “how flood-prone at magnitude X”, GFM answers “where and when it actually flooded”.

  • Temporal Coverage: None
  • Spatial Resolution: world, kenya — 3 arc-seconds (~90 m)

8.4.2 Data Producers & Custodians

  • Alliance of Bioversity International and CIAT (Peter Steward)
  • Source Cooperative / Radiant Earth Foundation - https://source.coop/nlebovits/jrc-glofas
  • European Commission Joint Research Centre (JRC) - https://data.jrc.ec.europa.eu/

8.4.3 Scientific Caveats & Guidance

[!WARNING] FEDERATED TO THE CLOUD-OPTIMIZED UPSTREAM (2026-09-18, following review on cdh-catalog#32). The full global product is already published as COGs with internal overviews under CC-BY-4.0 on Source Cooperative, at the same seven return periods and the same 3 arc-second resolution as the Atlas crop. The canonical asset in this record is therefore the global collection, not our subset. The Kenya crop is retained as a provisional second asset because the KE-ENSO Explorer currently reads it, but it carries no information the global collection lacks and the performance argument for keeping it is untested. Measuring that is the open task; retiring the duplicate is the likely outcome. Static modelled hazard (return-period depths); not an observed time series and not scenario-projected, so no temporal coverage is declared. Pair with the GFM observed extent for the “modelled vs observed” flood story.

8.5 kenya-flood-gfm: Kenya Observed Flood Extent (Copernicus GFM, Sentinel-1)

Category: Satellite  |  Publishing State: authored  |  License: Open Data

8.5.1 Summary

Observed flood extent for Kenya from the Copernicus Emergency Management Service Global Flood Monitoring (GFM) product, which classifies flooding from every Sentinel-1 SAR acquisition (ensemble of the DLR, TU Wien and LIST algorithms; permanent and reference water excluded). Delivered as ~111 m aggregates built directly from the 20 m SAR tiles: a monthly flood-occurrence layer with its valid-observation count, a rolling 3-month (seasonal) layer aligned to the KE-ENSO Explorer precipitation seasons, and a full-record history (occurrence frequency and ever-flooded footprint), 2018 to present. Replaces the MODIS Global Flood Database (ended 2018) with a cloud-penetrating, continuously updated SAR record. The 20 m per-acquisition archive is not materialised here (16,000+ acquisitions); it can be re-pulled from the EODC STAC on demand.

  • Temporal Coverage: 2018-01 to present (open-ended)
  • Spatial Resolution: kenya — 0.001 deg (~111 m); aggregated from 20 m Sentinel-1 tiles (a coarse pixel is flooded if any 20 m sub-pixel flooded)

8.5.2 Data Producers & Custodians

  • Alliance of Bioversity International and CIAT (Peter Steward)
  • Copernicus Emergency Management Service (CEMS) - https://global-flood.emergency.copernicus.eu/

8.5.3 Scientific Caveats & Guidance

[!WARNING] Attribution required: “Contains modified Copernicus Emergency Management Service information [year]”. Pixel coding of the flooded layers: 0 = not flooded, 1 = flooded, 255 = not observed (SAR gap or excluded) - 255 is absence of observation, NOT dry; the nobs layer gives the count of valid observations so no-flood is distinguishable from no-observation. Seasonal windows are named by the initials of their months and labelled by the year of their FIRST month (DJF_2018 = Dec 2018 to Feb 2019), so NDJ and DJF exist for 2018 to 2024 only. This differs from the CHIRPS seasonal record (africa-precipitation-monthly-seasonal), which labels NDJ and DJF by the year the window ends; align on those two windows with care. A ~7-year record measures recent observed flood occurrence, not return-period magnitude.

8.6 eastafrica-ndvi-modis: MODIS NDVI Seasonal Vegetation Condition (East Africa)

Category: Satellite  |  Publishing State: authored  |  License: Open Data

8.6.1 Summary

Normalized Difference Vegetation Index from MODIS MOD13Q1 (250 m, 16-day composites), averaged over the two East African rainy seasons - the long rains (MAM, March to May) and the short rains (OND, October to December) - for every year from 2000 to 2025, over an East Africa window centred on Kenya. Written as Cloud Optimized GeoTIFFs. The vegetation-condition layer of the KE-ENSO Explorer: greenness response to seasonal rainfall and ENSO phase.

  • Temporal Coverage: 2000 to 2025
  • Spatial Resolution: eastern-africa, kenya — ~250 m (0.0021 deg, MOD13Q1 native)

8.6.2 Data Producers & Custodians

  • Alliance of Bioversity International and CIAT (Peter Steward)
  • NASA LP DAAC / USGS EROS - https://lpdaac.usgs.gov/products/mod13q1v061/

8.6.3 Scientific Caveats & Guidance

[!WARNING] Source MODIS data are free and unrestricted; these aggregated COGs are released CC-BY-4.0 with attribution to NASA LP DAAC. NDVI ranges roughly -0.2 to 1.0 (higher = greener). Only the MAM and OND windows are published (no other seasons, no annual mean). Each file covers the full East Africa window (~275 MB); read with a bbox/overview to avoid whole-file downloads.

8.7 eastafrica-wrsi-fews: FEWS NET Water Requirement Satisfaction Index, Cropland and Rangeland (Kenya)

Category: Satellite  |  Publishing State: authored  |  License: Open Data

8.7.1 Summary

End-of-season Water Requirement Satisfaction Index (WRSI) for cropland (maize) and rangeland growing seasons from USGS FEWS NET, for the long rains (MAM) and short rains (OND) of every year from 2003, cropped to Kenya and written as Cloud Optimized GeoTIFFs. WRSI is the ratio of seasonal water supplied to crop or pasture water demand (0 to 100 percent); low values flag agricultural water deficit. The crop and rangeland monitoring layer of the KE-ENSO Explorer, on the same CHIRPS v3 precipitation backbone as the rainfall and SPEI layers.

  • Temporal Coverage: 2003 to present (open-ended)
  • Spatial Resolution: kenya — 0.1 deg (~11 km, FEWS NET WRSI grid)

8.7.2 Data Producers & Custodians

  • Alliance of Bioversity International and CIAT (Peter Steward)
  • USGS FEWS NET - https://earlywarning.usgs.gov/fews

8.7.3 Scientific Caveats & Guidance

[!WARNING] Cropland and rangeland use different water-demand parameterisations and growing-season definitions (FEWS NET region codes / end-of-season dekads), so the two products are not interchangeable. The {crop} x {season} cells map to FEWS NET East Africa region codes as follows (FEWS NET zip-product instructions, Table 1, 2025-02-03): rangeland OND = e1 (Sep-Jan), rangeland MAM = e2 (Feb-Jul), cropland OND = et (maize, Oct-Feb), cropland MAM = ee (maize, Mar-Nov). Not every season x year combination of the template exists: the most recent season is only published once FEWS NET releases its end-of-season product. PROVENANCE: objects published between 2026-08-18 and 2026-09-20 had the cropland and rangeland labels swapped (e1/e2 served as cropland, ek/et as rangeland); every object was replaced in place on 2026-09-20 and copies fetched before that date should be discarded.

8.8 kenya-flood-exposure-intersect: Kenya Flood x Exposure, Per-Sub-County Tables

Category: Satellite  |  Publishing State: authored  |  License: Open Data

8.8.1 Summary

Pre-computed flood x exposure statistics for Kenya’s 290 IEBC sub-counties (admin 2), as three small Parquet tables the KE-ENSO Explorer reads directly (DuckDB-WASM) instead of intersecting heavy rasters and vectors in the browser. Two hazard products - Copernicus GFM observed flood (per season x year, 2018 to 2025) and JRC modelled return-period flood hazard - are each crossed with population (WorldPop constrained 100 m grid, levelled to the official KNBS county totals), classified roads and the electricity transmission grid (length exposed) and health and school facilities (count exposed); a third table holds the per-sub-county denominators. Tables carry no geometry: join on adm2_pcode to the boundary record kenya-admin-codab.

  • Temporal Coverage: 2018 to 2025
  • Spatial Resolution: kenya — IEBC sub-counties (290 admin-2 units)

8.8.2 Data Producers & Custodians

  • Alliance of Bioversity International and CIAT (Peter Steward)

8.8.3 Scientific Caveats & Guidance

[!WARNING] Derived aggregate statistics; no input geometry is redistributed. Please attribute the inputs: Copernicus GFM and JRC Global Flood Maps (Copernicus / European Commission), WorldPop constrained population (CC-BY-4.0), OpenStreetMap roads and HOTOSM facilities ((c) OpenStreetMap contributors, ODbL), KPLC grid (CC0-1.0, energydata.info), IEBC COD-AB boundaries (OCHA, CC-BY-3.0-IGO). In the GFM table, observed_pct is the SAR-observed share of the sub-county in that season: low values mean sparse observation, not absence of flooding. Road and grid kilometres are cell-resolution approximations (in-cell line length within flooded cells). Every table also carries adm1_pcode, adm1_name and adm2_name label columns. POPULATION METHOD (issue #28), stated in full. Headcounts are NOT raw gridded pixel sums. Every published sub-county population is built as three explicit factors, each published as its own column:

[!WARNING] pop_adm2 = pop_adm2_grid x pop_scale_census x pop_growth_county

[!WARNING] (1) pop_adm2_grid - the 100 m gridded pixel sum, which supplies the SHARE of the county’s people

[!WARNING] living in that sub-county, and nothing else.

[!WARNING] (2) pop_scale_census = census county total / gridded county total - replaces the LEVEL of the

[!WARNING] gridded surface with the enumerated KNBS 2019 census, county by county. The gridded surfaces

[!WARNING] run about 17 per cent above the census (WorldPop constrained ~55.2 M, GRID3 ~55.9 M, census

[!WARNING] 47,564,296), so this factor is ~0.855 nationally but spans 0.324-1.394 across individual

[!WARNING] counties (measured on the published grid, 2026-09-17): WorldPop exceeds the census by up to

[!WARNING] threefold in some counties and falls below it in others. Any aggregate therefore moves by a

[!WARNING] weighted mix of county factors, not by the national one - flood-exposed population sits mostly

[!WARNING] in low-factor counties, so the GFM exposed total fell about 35 per cent where the national

[!WARNING] denominator fell 14 per cent. No time component.

[!WARNING] (3) pop_growth_county = target county total / census county total - the county’s proportional

[!WARNING] CHANGE OVER TIME, from the KNBS Vol XVI projections (record

[!WARNING] kenya-population-knbs-projections). Exactly 1.0 when the denominator is the census itself.

[!WARNING] County is the only level where any of this is defensible: COD-AB adm2 is 290 IEBC constituencies while the census reports 345 KNBS sub-counties, and only 183 names match (record kenya-population-knbs-census); KNBS does not project below county at all. FIXED-SHARE ASSUMPTION. Each sub-county’s share of its county is held constant at the gridded 2020 distribution, so every sub-county in a county moves by exactly that county’s percentage change. Sub-county-specific growth is NOT represented: a fast-urbanising sub-county and a depopulating one in the same county grow at the same rate here. Growth does vary between counties - the 2025 factors span 1.03 to 1.15 - but not within one. YEAR MATCHING (the published default from 2026-09-17). The GFM table records OBSERVED floods and carries a year, so a single fixed denominator is wrong in one direction or the other: the 2019 census undercounts a 2024 flood by roughly 9 per cent, and a 2025 projection overcounts a 2018 one by a similar margin. Each GFM row is therefore levelled against ITS OWN year’s county population, and pop_year records which year was used. Years the KNBS projections cover (2020 onward) use them; 2018 and 2019 fall back to the enumerated census, because nothing official projects backwards from the 2020 base and inventing a figure would be worse than stating the fallback. Consequence for consumers: pop_source VARIES BY ROW in the GFM table - “knbs-census-2019” for 2018-2019 and “knbs-projection-” thereafter - so do not read one value and assume it applies to the file. The JRC table is a return-period hazard with no event year, and the totals table is a static denominator, so both take a single stated reference year (pop_year, same on every row). TWO DEFINITIONS OF THE GROWTH FACTOR, recorded in pop_method:

[!WARNING] county-growth-from- - pop_growth_county = projection(year) / projection(base year,

[!WARNING] 2020). The enumerated census remains the level and KNBS supplies only the shape of change;

[!WARNING] national 2025 lands near 51.96 M because the ~2.6 per cent step KNBS puts between census night

[!WARNING] (August 2019) and its own 2020 base is deliberately not imported.

[!WARNING] county-level - pop_growth_county = projection(year) / census(2019), so the county total IS the

[!WARNING] published official projection and national 2025 is 53,330,964 - the figure a Kenyan

[!WARNING] counterpart would quote - at the cost of superseding the census anchor with the KNBS base-level

[!WARNING] revision.

[!WARNING] pop_source names the population vintage (knbs-census-2019, or knbs-projection-); pop_grid_source names the surface supplying the share. Nothing is discarded: the raw gridded figures remain in pop_exposed_grid and pop_total_grid. pop_pct is identical under every option - the factors cancel.

8.9 kenya-population-knbs-census: KNBS 2019 Census Population Counts by County and Sub-county (Kenya)

Category: Demographics  |  Publishing State: authored  |  License: Open Data

8.9.1 Summary

Enumerated population from the 2019 Kenya Population and Housing Census (KNBS), as admin-level tables: national, the 47 counties keyed on IEBC COD-AB adm1_pcode, the 345 KNBS sub-county rows in the Bureau’s own sub-county universe, and a county x 5-year age group x sex table. The official denominator for anything the KE-ENSO Explorer presents to a Kenyan audience: the census total is 47,564,296, where the two gridded surfaces the Explorer also carries (kenya-population-worldpop 55.2 M, kenya-population-grid3 55.9 M) sit about 17 per cent higher.

  • Temporal Coverage: 2019
  • Spatial Resolution: kenya — counties (47 admin-1 units); a sub-county table is included in the KNBS universe

8.9.2 Data Producers & Custodians

  • Alliance of Bioversity International and CIAT (Peter Steward)
  • Kenya National Bureau of Statistics (KNBS) - https://www.knbs.or.ke/

8.9.3 Scientific Caveats & Guidance

Attribution is given as a courtesy though CC0 does not require it. HOW THE PORTAL PAGES, AND WHY EARLIER COUNTS WERE WRONG. The HTML results table pages by an offset in the URL path (/site/market_search/), not by the page= query parameter, so one wide request returns only the first per_page rows. The Excel export honours per_page and returns a whole window in one file. The harvester pulls one export per species-year and checks the row count against the portal’s own pagination total; on 2026-10-23 that was 116,634 rows across the four species. WHOLESALE IS THE LIVESTOCK PRICE. 99.8 percent of live-animal quotes sit in the portal’s Wholesale column, which is the per-head price at the livestock market; the Retail column is essentially empty for animals. The explorer therefore serves these as wholesale prices, distinct from the FEWS NET retail goat and cattle series it already shows. THE 2021-05-24 BULK BACKFILL. The portal’s history starts on 2021-05-24, and that single date carries 11,079 quotes, about one tenth of the whole archive. It is the upload date of a backfill, not a market day. Those rows are kept in the market-day file with the flag launch_day_bulk and are excluded from the monthly medians. KAMIS cannot reach the 2011 or 2017 drought analogues; the FEWS NET series remains the long-run record. DIRTY ROWS ARE FLAGGED, NOT DROPPED. A county literally named test (345 quotes), 3,203 exact duplicate submissions, 67 non-positive prices and 591 order-of-magnitude outliers against the same market-day median are all present in the market-day file with a qc_flag, and absent from the monthly medians. County spellings are canonicalised to the 47-county key (Tana-River to Tana River, Homa-bay to Homa Bay, Muranga to Murang’a); the portal spelling is kept in county_raw. COVERAGE IS UNEVEN AND THINNING. Reporting peaked in 2022 (about 40,000 quotes) and fell to a few thousand a year by 2025; camel quotes stop at 2025-11-24. Absence of a market-month is a gap in reporting, not a zero price. Grade and breed are enumerator judgements with no published grading standard, so grade comparisons across markets are indicative only. NO COORDINATES. The portal names the market and county only; the file is plain parquet, not GeoParquet. OBFUSCATED DELIVERY, BY CHOICE. As a courtesy to the Ministry while reuse terms are sought, the market-day file is published to the explorer only as an AES-256-GCM container (market_prices_kamis.parquet.enc) that the explorer decrypts in the browser; the monthly medians it displays are derived aggregates. This stops casual download and crawling. It is not access control: a static page must hold whatever decrypts it, so a determined reader can recover the bytes. The plain file is not committed to the repository and no bulk download is offered.

8.22.3 Scientific Caveats & Guidance

[!WARNING] LICENCE CONFIRMED 2026-09-18, AND IT CONSTRAINS DOWNSTREAM USE. IPC states that all IPC results are licensed CC BY-NC-SA 3.0 IGO. Two consequences that do not apply elsewhere in the Atlas stack: NON-COMMERCIAL, so IPC figures cannot travel into a commercially licensed or commercially used product; and SHARE-ALIKE, so any adapted work built on them must carry the same licence, which conflicts with the CC-BY-4.0 most Atlas products use. Citing IPC phases and linking out is unencumbered; folding them into a derived Atlas layer is not. The IPC Analysis Platform is hosted by FAO and governed by its terms of use. A PHASE IS A CLASSIFICATION, NOT A MEASUREMENT. Phases 1-5 come from an expert consensus process over converging evidence, so they are ordinal and are NOT comparable across analysis rounds in the way a measured index is: methodology, evidence base and analyst panel all change between rounds. Do not difference phases between rounds to derive a trend, and do not interpolate between analyses. Kenya analyses are conducted twice a year - long rains (July-August) and short rains (January-February) - and the IPC process is hosted within NDMA.

8.23.3 Scientific Caveats & Guidance

[!WARNING] LICENCE - AN EXPLICIT WORKING ASSUMPTION, NOT A GRANT. No party has published terms for these sheets. The Atlas treats them as open pending evidence otherwise, a decision taken by the data owner on 2026-09-18 and recorded here so it is visible rather than implied: LicenseRef-KE-Gov- Statistics-Assumed-Open means “no rights stated, treated as open”, NOT “the publishers granted an open licence”. Anyone relying on these figures commercially should confirm with the publishers first. The evidence behind the assumption follows. Every page of a sheet was read and the raw PDF scanned: there is NO copyright notice, NO “all rights reserved” and NO ISBN. That is materially better footing than the KNBS census projections (record kenya-population-knbs-projections, whose Volume XVI reserves rights in terms) - but absence of a notice is NOT a grant, and copyright subsists automatically. Worse, there is no single party whose terms could be pointed at: these are a six-way co-publication. The KNBS Open License Agreement does NOT reach them, being scoped to data published on the KNBS Open Data Platform, while these are PDFs on knbs.or.ke - the same gap already established for Volume XVI. UN Women co-publishes under the Women Count programme and normally releases CC-BY-3.0-IGO, which is a signal and not a statement. Settle with UN Women Kenya or the State Department for Gender before anything derived from these is published. EDITION. The October 2025 set, all 47 county sheets, uploaded to knbs.or.ke in April and May 2026. Not the 2019 ten-county pilot, and not a mix. A static cross-section, not an ongoing series. SIZE. The Atlas extraction yields 1,578 rows: 35 indicator series across 24 stable codes, long format, county level only. VINTAGE IS NOT UNIFORM AND IS NOT RECOVERABLE FROM THE SHEETS AS EXTRACTED. Indicators mix 2022 KDHS, 2019 census-derived figures and 2022 election results. The Atlas extraction strips any trailing year from indicator labels, so no per-indicator reference year survives in the derived table. Treating the whole table as “2022” is wrong for some indicators. BLANK IS NOT ZERO. The extraction serves a series only where at least 40 of 47 counties agree with the modal national value; non-matching county rows are dropped individually. So a served series can be silently missing up to seven counties, and a missing county-series is a parse or gate drop - NEVER an observed zero. Only 10 of 35 series reach all 47 counties; the worst is households with an elderly member at 40/47. Per-county row counts run 22-35. COVERAGE IS ASYMMETRIC WITHIN AN INDICATOR. One code can have 47 counties for male and 46 for female. Do not compute a sex ratio or a gender gap without checking both sides are present for that county. CHART-ONLY INDICATORS ARE ABSENT BY METHOD. Antenatal-visit and vaccination charts and internet usage are dropped by the gate. Their absence reflects the extraction, not the source. LABELS ARE NOT DEFINITIONS. The canonical label is the longest string seen across the 47 sheets and several remain truncated mid-sentence. Do not lift them into variable descriptions. COLUMN-ORDER FRAGILITY. Values are read Kenya-then-County from the printed header. A future edition that flips that header would swap every value silently; the coverage gate catches it only if the national column also becomes inconsistent.
