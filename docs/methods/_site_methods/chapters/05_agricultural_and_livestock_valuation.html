6  Agricultural Production & Economic Valuation

7 Agricultural Production & Economic Valuation

This chapter formalizes the agricultural production anomaly functions, historical crop yield detrending, and the county economic capital valuation engine. It documents KNBS National Agriculture Production Report (NAPR) extractions, HarvestStat historical yields, and the subcounty spatial weight apportionment model under Beta review governance.

7.1.1 Scientific Purpose & Context

7.2 Figure 1.2: Subnational Agricultural Capital Valuation & Subcounty Spatial Apportionment

Section in Platform: Section 1.2  |  Target Identifier: fig_1_2  |  Output Type: Chart

Policy / Scientific Question: What agricultural and livestock capital is exposed to climate extremes in the county, how much of it is standing herd wealth as opposed to annual harvest and offtake value, and how is it distributed across the county’s constituencies?

7.2.1 Scientific Purpose & Context

Climate risk screening needs a value at risk. A drought that kills a fifth of a county’s cattle and a drought that cuts its maize harvest by a fifth are different events: the first destroys capital that takes years of breeding to rebuild, the second destroys one year’s income. Global gridded value-of-production surfaces, including the Atlas-wide MapSPAM crop and Gridded Livestock of the World (GLW4) livestock layers expressed in constant 2015 international dollars, conflate these two things. They value livestock by annual output (meat offtake and milk), so a pastoral county’s wealth is represented by a small flow number while the herd itself, the asset that drought actually erodes, does not appear. In the earliest version of this figure that approach valued Marsabit’s cattle at about 31 million US dollars and omitted camels entirely, which no pastoral planner would recognise.

Figure 1.2 therefore rebuilds the county portfolio from Kenya’s statutory statistics and separates two quantities explicitly. Standing stock is the replacement value of the live herd: head counts from the 2019 Kenya Population and Housing Census and the Kenya National Bureau of Statistics (KNBS) National Agricultural Production Report (NAPR), valued at county sale prices with a capital-share adjustment for herd structure. Annual flow is the value of what the county produces in a year: crop harvests at farm-gate prices, livestock offtake and milk, and official livestock products (eggs, honey, hides, poultry meat). Both are reported in constant 2021 Kenyan shillings with a fixed 2021 conversion to US dollars, so that changes between years reflect physical quantities, not price inflation or exchange-rate movements. A portfolio chart shows the composition by commodity, and a constituency view apportions every county total across the 290 parliamentary constituencies (IEBC sub-counties) with mass-preserving spatial weights.

The output is a screening estimate of exposure, built for comparing counties and sub-counties and for sizing contingency planning. It is published under Beta review governance: livestock prices in the arid and semi-arid counties are annual medians from National Drought Management Authority (NDMA) county drought early warning bulletins used under research fair dealing while formal licensing is sought, so the asset tables are shown for expert review, are not released as open data and carry no bulk download.

7.2.2 Mathematical Formulation & Algorithmic Approach

Baseline Reference Period: Valuation base year 2021 (constant 2021 KES; USD at the 2021 annual average rate); crops 2019 to 2024, livestock and products 2021

Tier 1, quantities. Crop production by county and year comes from the KNBS NAPR 2023/24 and 2024/25 editions (deterministic PDF table extraction with additivity gates), supplemented by Agriculture and Food Authority (AFA) county returns for paddy rice and by Horticultural Crops Directorate county values for vegetables and fruits in 2021. Livestock head counts by county and species (cattle, camels, goats, sheep, donkeys, pigs, poultry) come from the 2019 Kenya Population and Housing Census (KPHC) Volume IV household livestock tables and the NAPR livestock population tables for 2021; the dairy share of the cattle herd is taken from the KPHC exotic-dairy count and, in highland counties only, scaled so that the national dairy herd matches the NAPR total. Livestock product quantities (milk, eggs, honey, hides, poultry meat) are the KNBS 2021 county figures.

Tier 2, prices. The base year is 2021 and all prices are 2021 Kenyan shillings (KES) at farm-gate or primary-market level. Crop prices follow a documented preference order: AFA Yearbook 2021 farm-gate prices first (coffee on a cherry-equivalent basis, tea at the Kenya Tea Development Agency green-leaf rate), then KNBS Economic Survey Table 7.4 average gross prices to farmers, then MapSPAM 2020 FAOSTAT-derived producer prices as the last resort. Live-animal sale prices in the 23 arid and semi-arid counties are 2019 to 2021 annual medians of the NDMA county drought early warning bulletins; elsewhere they are 2021 Kenya Agricultural Market Information System (KAMIS) medians and literature values for dairy cattle, donkeys, pigs and poultry. Milk is priced at county milk prices. Every parameter and price carries source identifiers resolvable in a sha256-verified source registry, and the build fails if any identifier is missing.

Tier 3, livestock parameters. Standing stock for cattle, goats and sheep is head count times a capital share of 0.75 times the sale price; the capital share reflects that a herd’s immatures and breeding females are not all saleable at the quoted price of a mature animal. Camels are valued at the NDMA sale price times an empirical herd-structure ratio (1.39 in Marsabit, 0.99 in Isiolo, 1.17 nationally). Annual flow is head count times an annual offtake rate times sale price (cattle 0.12 in arid and semi-arid zones and 0.08 in the highlands; camels 0.04; goats 0.16; sheep 0.14) plus milk litres times milk price, where cattle milk is dairy head times 800 L per head per year plus non-dairy head times a zone coefficient (55 L in arid, 100 L in semi-arid and highland zones), and camel milk is 180 L per head per year. Zone coefficients derive from the Behnke and Muthami (2011) audit of Kenyan livestock contribution to GDP, cross-checked against the primary sources, with goat milk from Field (1985). Counties are assigned to arid, semi-arid or highland zones from the 2015 Ending Drought Emergencies county list.

Tier 4, currency. Flows and stocks are converted to US dollars at the KNBS Economic Survey 2021 annual average of 109.65 KES per USD. The rate is fixed to the base year so that the dollar series is a constant-price series; a current rate is available only as an explicit user override. The legacy constant 2015 international dollar layers from MapSPAM and GLW4 are retained for comparison with the Atlas-wide exposure product but do not enter the county totals.

Tier 5, subcounty apportionment. Each county total for a commodity is distributed across its IEBC parliamentary constituencies in proportion to a spatial prior specific to that commodity. For crops and horticulture the prior is MapSPAM 2020 v1r2 physical area for the matching crop (or crop group), summed from the 5 arc-minute grid cells whose centroids fall inside each constituency. For livestock and livestock products the prior is the KPHC 2019 household livestock count for the species (or species group) by constituency, obtained by crosswalking census sub-counties to IEBC constituencies with the GLW4 density surface as the reference layer. Where a prior sums to zero within a county (12 percent of crop rows, under 1 percent of livestock rows) the constituency land-area share is used instead and the row is flagged as a fallback. Shares are gated to sum to one within each county and commodity to within 1e-12, and the apportioned values are gated to re-aggregate exactly to the county totals.

7.2.2.1 Methodological Rationale

Why constant base-year prices. The purpose of the figure is to expose physical production and herd capital to climate hazard, so the valuation must not move when prices move. Kenyan farm-gate prices and the shilling both shifted sharply over the data window (the shilling went from 109.65 per US dollar in 2021 to 139.85 in 2023, and KNBS revised the 2022 maize price upward by 35 percent between editions). Valuing every year’s quantities at the same 2021 prices and the same 2021 exchange rate means that a change in the series is a change in tonnes or head, which is what a drought or flood alters. The 2021 base year was chosen because it is the latest year for which KNBS county livestock populations, the Economic Survey price tables, the AFA yearbook and the NDMA bulletin price medians all overlap.

Why stock and flow are separated. Drought mortality and distress sales erode the herd, which is a capital stock; a poor harvest erodes one year’s output, which is a flow. Reporting only flow, as the gridded international-dollar layers do, makes pastoral counties look poor and under-states what a multi-season drought puts at risk; reporting only stock makes crop counties look poor and ignores the income shock. The capital-share parameter corrects a second conflation: multiplying every head by the sale price of a mature animal overstates herd value because a herd is mostly females and immatures, so the 0.75 share, derived from empirical Kenyan herd demographic structures, brings the stock value to a replacement basis. Camels receive a species-specific ratio because their herd structure and price structure differ from those of cattle and small stock.

Why mass-preserving dasymetric apportionment. County totals are statutory; constituency totals are not published. The honest way to produce a sub-county figure is to keep the county total fixed and distribute it with the best available spatial evidence of where the commodity is, which is the pycnophylactic (mass-preserving) property of dasymetric mapping. The priors are chosen to be commodity-specific so that maize follows maize area and camels follow camel counts, not population or land area. MapSPAM and GLW4 enter here only as spatial shape, never as level, so their known biases in absolute value (which motivated the rebuild) do not propagate into the totals.

Why Beta review governance. The NDMA bulletin prices are copyright NDMA with no open licence; they are used as derived annual medians under research fair dealing while permission is sought. Until that permission exists the assets cannot be redistributed, so the module carries a Beta Review Edition banner, provides no bulk download, and is excluded from the open Atlas data store and the Comprehensive Data Hub. The values are screening estimates for exposure comparison and contingency sizing; they are not calibrated for use as the basis of fiscal transfers, insurance payouts or compensation without further validation against county-level market and herd surveys.

Governing Mathematical Formulations:

County crop flow (gross value of production at constant 2021 farm-gate prices). Q is county production in tonnes from KNBS NAPR (plus AFA rice and HCD horticulture); the price for each crop is taken from the first available source in the preference order.

\[ F^{\text{crop}}_{c,y} = \sum_{i=1}^{I} Q_{c,i,y}\; P_{i}^{2021}, \qquad P_i^{2021} \in \{\text{AFA farm-gate} \succ \text{KNBS ES Table 7.4} \succ \text{FAOSTAT via MapSPAM}\} \]

County livestock standing stock (herd replacement capital). H is head count (KPHC 2019 and NAPR 2021), kappa is the capital share or camel herd-structure ratio, and the sale price is the NDMA 2019 to 2021 median in ASAL counties or the KAMIS 2021 median elsewhere.

\[ S_{c} = \sum_{j} H_{c,j}\; \kappa_j\; P^{\text{sale}}_{c,j}, \qquad \kappa_j = \begin{cases} 0.75 & j \in \{\text{cattle}, \text{goats}, \text{sheep}\} \\ \rho^{\text{camel}}_{c} & j = \text{camels} \\ 1 & \text{otherwise} \end{cases} \]

County livestock annual flow (offtake plus milk). r is the annual offtake rate by species and zone z(c); M is milk litres with the dairy herd at 800 L per head and the non-dairy herd at the zone coefficient (55 L arid, 100 L semi-arid and highland); camel milk is 180 L per head.

\[ F^{\text{live}}_{c} = \sum_{j} H_{c,j}\; r_{j,z(c)}\; P^{\text{sale}}_{c,j} \;+\; \sum_{j} M_{c,j}\; P^{\text{milk}}_{c}, \qquad M_{c,\text{cattle}} = H^{\text{dairy}}_{c}\cdot 800 + \left(H_{c,\text{cattle}} - H^{\text{dairy}}_{c}\right)\cdot m_{z(c)} \]

County livestock product flow and portfolio totals. Products (eggs, honey, hides, poultry meat) use KNBS 2021 county quantities at the KNBS 2021 national unit value; the dollar conversion is fixed at the 2021 annual average rate.

\[ F^{\text{prod}}_{c} = \sum_{g} Q_{c,g}\; \bar{P}^{\text{KE}}_{g}, \qquad F_c = F^{\text{crop}}_{c} + F^{\text{live}}_{c} + F^{\text{prod}}_{c}, \qquad V^{\text{USD}} = \frac{V^{\text{KES}}}{109.65} \]

Subcounty apportionment shares (mass-preserving dasymetric weights). Weights are commodity-specific; where the prior sums to zero within a county the constituency land-area share is substituted and the row flagged as a fallback.

\[ w_{sc,i} = \frac{A_{sc,i}}{\sum_{k \in c} A_{k,i}} \ \ (\text{crops: MapSPAM physical area}), \qquad w_{sc,j} = \frac{H^{\text{KPHC}}_{sc,j}}{\sum_{k \in c} H^{\text{KPHC}}_{k,j}} \ \ (\text{livestock: census head}), \qquad \sum_{sc \in c} w_{sc,\cdot} = 1 \]

Apportioned constituency values and county-sum identity. The build gates that apportioned values re-aggregate exactly to the statutory county totals.

\[ S_{sc} = \sum_{j} w_{sc,j}\, S_{c,j}, \qquad F_{sc} = \sum_{i} w_{sc,i}\, F_{c,i} + \sum_{j} w_{sc,j}\, F_{c,j}, \qquad \sum_{sc \in c} S_{sc} = S_c, \quad \sum_{sc \in c} F_{sc} = F_c \]

7.2.4 Methodological Assumptions & Analytical Limitations

  • Beta Review Edition governance: livestock prices for the 23 ASAL counties derive from NDMA county drought early warning bulletins used under research fair dealing pending formal NDMA licensing. The asset tables are provided for expert review only, carry no bulk download, and are not published to the open Atlas data store or the Comprehensive Data Hub.
  • The values are macro-level exposure screening estimates. They are suitable for comparing counties and constituencies and for sizing contingency plans; they are not calibrated for direct use in fiscal transfers, index insurance payouts or compensation schedules without validation against county market and herd surveys.
  • Livestock head counts are 2019 census household counts and 2021 NAPR populations. Pastoral herds are mobile and partly enumerated away from the household, and herd sizes changed substantially through the 2020 to 2023 drought; the 2021 baseline predates most of that mortality.
  • The capital share (0.75), camel herd-structure ratios, offtake rates and milk coefficients are zone-level parameters from audited literature, not county surveys. Each carries a documented range in the parameter file; the point estimates are used in the figure and the ranges are not propagated as uncertainty bands.
  • Milk coefficients rest on Behnke and Muthami (2011) field audits and on Field (1985) for small stock, with the dairy-herd scaling applied in highland counties only; dry-county dairy herds are deliberately left unscaled because national scaling inflated them 1.3 to 13 times.
  • Crops are valued for 2019 to 2024 but livestock and products for 2021 only, so the portfolio is a 2021 snapshot and the crop time series should not be read as a trend in total assets.
  • Crop prices mix three sources with different bases (farm-gate, gross price to farmers, FAOSTAT producer price). Horticulture enters as county-reported values rather than as quantity times price.
  • MapSPAM 2020 physical area is itself a modelled allocation of national and sub-national statistics to a 10 km grid, so crop apportionment inherits its allocation errors; in heterogeneous pastoral ecotones the cropland prior and the census livestock prior both carry classification error, and 12 percent of crop rows fall back to land-area shares.
  • The KPHC 2019 census sub-counties do not coincide with IEBC constituencies; the crosswalk introduces boundary error in counties where the two geographies diverge.
  • Fisheries, forestry, draught power, manure and the non-market services of livestock are outside the valuation; the flow is a gross value, not value added, and input costs are not deducted.
  • County aggregations rely on official polygon boundaries (IEBC constituencies via UN OCHA COD-AB); historical boundary changes are not back-cast.

7.3 Figure 4.1: Dual-Season Ocean Drivers vs. Agricultural & Livestock Output

Section in Platform: Section 4  |  Target Identifier: fig_3_1  |  Output Type: Chart

Policy / Scientific Question: In the years covered by official county statistics, how did crop production, livestock numbers and livestock product value move relative to their typical level, and what were the two rainy seasons and the ocean drivers doing in the seasons that fed each year?

7.3.1 Scientific Purpose & Context

Annual agricultural output in Kenya is fed by two rainy seasons, and harvests lag the rains that grew them. Figure 4.1 lines up the official county statistics from the Kenya National Bureau of Statistics (KNBS) National Agricultural Production Report (NAPR) with the seasons that fed each year, so a planner can see whether a weak year followed a failed season, a double failure, or neither.

The upper panel plots the selected crops, livestock species or livestock products for the county as an index relative to each item’s median over the surveyed years (100 = typical), or as raw values. The lower strips show, for each year, the seasonal rainfall anomaly in percent of the 1991–2020 mean and the seasonal ocean driver values (Relative Oceanic Niño Index, Dipole Mode Index and the Western-V index) against their phase thresholds, with an optional vegetation (NDVI) overlay. For crops the year is a harvest year, and it is paired with the preceding Short Rains (OND of the previous calendar year) and the concurrent Long Rains (MAM). Livestock and product years are paired with the same calendar year’s MAM and OND, with lagged values available in the table view.

The figure is a descriptive, record-linking tool. The KNBS county series currently span only 2019 to 2024 for crops and 2021 to 2023 for livestock and products, so it supports reading individual recent years against their seasons, for example the 2020 to 2022 multi-season drought, rather than estimating how output responds to ocean drivers in general. Longer-run crop responses are examined with HarvestStat county series elsewhere in Section 4.

7.3.2 Mathematical Formulation & Algorithmic Approach

Baseline Reference Period: Output indexed to the median of the surveyed years; rainfall anomalies against the 1991–2020 WMO standard normal

Output series. For the selected county the figure assembles three groups from the KNBS NAPR extractions: crop production in tonnes (with paddy rice from Agriculture and Food Authority county returns), livestock head counts by species, and livestock product value in Kenyan shillings, excluding values flagged as suspect during extraction. The user chooses a group and up to a few items (by default the two leading food crops). Only positive values are plotted. The years shown are the years with data for the selected items: currently 2019 to 2024 for crops and 2021 to 2023 for livestock and products.

Median index. Each item’s typical level is the median of its positive values over the surveyed years. In the default view each value is plotted as an index, 100 times the value divided by that median, so that items with very different units share one axis. The table view reports the percentage deviation from the median and flags deviations of at least +15 percent and at most −20 percent. No trend is fitted or removed.

Season pairing. For crops, year Y is a harvest year paired with OND of year Y−1 and MAM of year Y, following the national convention that Short Rains crops are harvested in the following calendar year. For livestock and products, year Y is paired with MAM and OND of the same year, and the table adds the preceding seasons as lagged columns.

Seasonal climate strips. Rainfall is shown as the percentage anomaly of the county-mean CHIRPS v3 seasonal total against its 1991–2020 mean; SPEI-3 is available as an alternative. The ocean drivers are the CPC RONI for the season (falling back to a seasonal mean of monthly values when the seasonal record is missing), the mean of the available monthly HadISST DMI values over the season (ERSST where HadISST is missing), and the mean of the standardized Western-V index over March to May. Each driver is drawn against its phase thresholds: ±0.5 °C for RONI, ±0.4 °C for DMI and ±0.5 standard deviations for Western-V. The bars are coloured by season, not by phase.

7.3.2.1 Methodological Rationale

Why a median index rather than a detrended anomaly. Detrending needs a series long enough to estimate a trend separately from the shocks of interest. The KNBS county series are four to six years long, and a linear trend fitted to them would absorb a drought year as readily as a real trend in area or productivity. The median of the surveyed years is the most robust typical level available for so short a record: it is not pulled by one extreme year, and it makes items in tonnes, head and shillings comparable on one axis. Long-run teleconnection effects on yield are better examined with the multi-decadal HarvestStat county series shown elsewhere in Section 4, which are long enough to separate trend from shocks.

Why explicit season pairing. Placing an annual harvest beside same-year seasonal shading invites a misreading, because the Short Rains of year Y are harvested in year Y+1. The figure therefore pairs each crop harvest year with the preceding OND and the concurrent MAM, labels the axis as harvest year, and draws the two seasons as separate bars, so the lag is part of the visual grammar rather than a footnote. Livestock numbers and products are census and survey-year quantities with no single harvest date, so they are paired with the same year’s seasons and the lagged seasons are reported alongside.

Why raw driver thresholds here. The strips are a reading aid placed against the physical index values that county planners encounter in bulletins and outlooks, so they use the conventional raw thresholds for each index rather than the standardized classes used for the contingency analysis in Figure 3.2.

Governing Mathematical Formulations:

Median index of output. V is production (t), head count or product value (KSh) for item j; the set of years is the item’s surveyed years in the county.

\[ I_{j,Y} = 100\,\frac{V_{j,Y}}{\tilde{V}_j}, \qquad \tilde{V}_j = \operatorname{median}\{V_{j,y} : y \in \mathcal{Y}_j,\ V_{j,y} > 0\} \]

Deviation from the median (table view). Asymmetric flag thresholds highlight shortfalls more readily than surpluses.

\[ \delta_{j,Y} = 100\,\frac{V_{j,Y} - \tilde{V}_j}{\tilde{V}_j}, \qquad \text{flag: } \delta \ge +15\%\ \text{or}\ \delta \le -20\% \]

Season pairing by output type. Crop years are harvest years; Short Rains crops are harvested in the following calendar year.

\[ \text{crops: } Y \leftrightarrow \left(\text{OND}_{Y-1},\ \text{MAM}_{Y}\right), \qquad \text{livestock and products: } Y \leftrightarrow \left(\text{MAM}_{Y},\ \text{OND}_{Y}\right)\ \text{with lagged } \left(\text{MAM}_{Y-1},\ \text{OND}_{Y-1}\right) \]

7.3.4 Methodological Assumptions & Analytical Limitations

  • The record is very short. Crops have at most six surveyed years and livestock and products three, so the figure cannot estimate a response to ocean drivers; it only places individual years beside their seasons. No correlation, regression or significance test is computed.
  • The median of four to six years is itself uncertain and is pulled toward whatever conditions dominated the surveyed years; for 2019 to 2024 that period includes the 2020 to 2022 drought, so the “typical” level may be lower than a longer-run normal.
  • Output is not detrended, and changes in planted area, herd management or KNBS survey coverage between editions are not separated from weather effects.
  • Crop production mixes MAM-grown and OND-grown harvests in one annual figure, so a single harvest year reflects both seasons in unknown proportions that vary by county and crop.
  • Livestock head counts and product values are stock and survey quantities whose response to drought is lagged and cumulative; pairing them with same-year seasons understates the effect of earlier seasons, which the lagged table columns only partly address.
  • Values flagged as suspect in the NAPR product tables are excluded, and blanks are not treated as zeros, so some items have gaps.
  • The optional NDVI overlay is drawn only for surveyed years, so the preceding Short Rains value is often unavailable for the first year shown.
  • Driver strips use seasonal means of whatever monthly values are available, without a completeness check, and the Western-V index is defined only for MAM.
  • County aggregations rely on official polygon boundaries from the UN OCHA Common Operational Datasets (COD-AB).
